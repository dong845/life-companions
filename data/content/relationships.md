# Relationship reflection — content library (frameworks, curated)

The deep-lookup layer for `modules/relationships.md`. Real, evidence-based
frameworks used as **reflective lenses** to interpret logged incidents — never
therapy, never diagnosis, never a verdict on the absent partner.

> **Framing contract.** These are lenses for *self-reflection and clearer requests*,
> not tools to diagnose or accuse a partner. Attachment style is not a horoscope;
> "Horseman/gaslighting/avoidant" are not labels to weaponize. Safety triage (§F)
> runs FIRST and always. When things are stuck or unsafe, route to a human
> professional — know the ceiling.

Render example phrasing in the user's `locale`. One-line summary of the method:
**attachment = why · Gottman = what's happening + what to do · NVC = how to say it ·
love languages = held loosely · all gated behind safety triage.**

---

## A. Attachment — the "why" (a lens, not a label)

Modern research treats attachment as **two continuous dimensions**, not 4 boxes:
- **Anxiety** — fear of rejection/abandonment; needs reassurance; hypervigilant to
  a partner's mood.
- **Avoidance** — discomfort with closeness/dependence; self-reliant; withdraws
  under stress.

The four "styles" are quadrants — read them as *tendencies in a moment*:

| Style | Anxiety | Avoidance | Under stress tends to… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Secure** | low | low | approach, state the need directly, soothe & be soothed |
| **Anxious/preoccupied** | high | low | seek reassurance, protest, read into small cues |
| **Avoidant/dismissing** | low | high | pull away, minimize needs, go quiet, value independence |
| **Fearful-avoidant** | high | high | want closeness *and* fear it; push-pull, mixed signals |

**How to use it (always as tendency, never diagnosis):**
- Say *"an anxious-leaning reaction here"*, NEVER *"you're anxious-attached"* / *"he's avoidant"*.
- Reframe conflict as a **strategy clash, not villainy**: the **pursue–withdraw trap**
  — one escalates for connection, the other retreats for safety, each triggering
  the other. Neither is the bad guy.
- Translate behavior → the **need/fear under it**: withdrawal is often
  self-protection, not indifference; nagging/protest is often a bid for
  reassurance, not control.
- **Not fixed** — attachment shifts with experience ("earned security"). No determinism.
- Infer tendencies **passively from the log over time** (recurring "texted twice,
  panicked when no reply" → anxious-leaning), rather than quizzing.

## B. Gottman — what's happening + what to do (most actionable)

**The Four Horsemen** (corrosive patterns; contempt is the strongest breakup
predictor) — each has an antidote to coach toward:

| Horseman | Looks like | Antidote |
|---|---|---|
| **Criticism** | "you *always*/*never*…", attacking character | **Gentle start-up**: "I felt X when Y; I need Z" (behavior, not the person) |
| **Contempt** | mockery, eye-roll, sarcasm, superiority | build **appreciation/respect**; speak your own feeling/need |
| **Defensiveness** | counter-attack, "it's not me, it's you", victim | **take some responsibility**, even a small part |
| **Stonewalling** | shutting down, silent treatment (often flooding) | **self-soothe**, take a 20+ min break, then return |

- **Bids for connection** — small requests for attention ("look at this", a sigh,
  a touch). Responses: **turning toward / away / against**. Daily erosion lives in
  missed bids — flag them in the log.
- **Repair attempts** — any de-escalating move ("let me try again", humor, "sorry,
  that came out wrong"). Thriving couples aren't better at avoiding conflict —
  they're better at **making and *accepting*** repairs. Coach both.
- **5:1 ratio** — stable couples run ~5 positive : 1 negative during conflict. A
  repair only lands if there's goodwill banked underneath. Bank positives.
- **Flooding** — when heart-rate spikes, no productive talk is possible; a timeout
  is a skill, not avoidance.

*Validity:* the descriptive patterns are robust; the "predict divorce 90%+"
figures were fit on their own data — **use the patterns, drop the fortune-telling.**

## C. NVC — how to say it (a rewriting tool)

Rosenberg's **OFNR** turns a heated entry into a sayable sentence:
1. **Observation** — the fact, no evaluation. ("当你吃饭时一直在看手机…")
2. **Feeling** — own emotion, not disguised blame. ("…我觉得有点孤单。")
   ⚠️ "我觉得你不在乎我" is a *thought/accusation*, not a feeling — catch this.
3. **Need** — the universal need underneath. ("…我需要一点连接感 / 被放在心上。")
4. **Request** — specific, present, doable, **not a demand**. ("下次吃饭我们能不能把手机放一边？")

Maps 1:1 onto Gottman's gentle start-up and attachment's "name the need". Use it as
*"here's how you could say this"*, a scaffold — not a claimed cure. Complementary
micro-skills: soft start-up, timeout when flooded, repair phrases, reflective
listening ("我听到的是…，对吗？").

## D. Love languages — held loosely (weak validity)

Chapman's five (words / quality time / acts of service / touch / gifts) are a useful
**vocabulary**, **not** an empirically supported theory: people value all five, the
categories overlap, and *matching* doesn't reliably predict satisfaction —
*expressing care in any form* does. Frame as a **preference menu / conversation
starter** ("这五种被爱的方式，哪种最戳你？"), never as a fixed type or "mismatch =
doomed". Honest takeaway: **express affection generously & variously, and ask your
partner what they actually value.**

## E. The anecdote → reflection pipeline (run internally, then surface balanced)

0. **Safety triage FIRST (§F).** If red flags → it's a *safety* situation, not a
   *communication* one. Switch modes; safety.md overrides.
1. **Separate observation from story.** What *happened* (behaviors/words) vs the
   *story* about it ("他不回我 = 他不在乎"). Most anecdotes are 20% event, 80%
   interpretation. Reflect the seam back gently.
2. **Steelman both sides.** Hold ≥2 charitable hypotheses for the partner ("went
   quiet" = contempt/stonewalling? OR flooding/self-protection? OR an unrelated bad
   day?). Don't resolve prematurely.
3. **Name the pattern with a lens** — Attachment (pursue–withdraw? whose fear?),
   Gottman (any Horsemen from *either* side? a missed bid? absent repair?), NVC
   (the unmet need under the feeling).
4. **Base-rate check.** One bad evening ≠ a pattern. Ask the log: has it recurred?
   Trajectory > any single event. The main guard against over-dramatizing.
5. **Reflect, then offer options (not verdicts).** Validate the *feeling* ("难受很
   合理") without endorsing the *interpretation as fact*. Offer 2–3 concrete moves:
   a repair phrase, an NVC sentence, a self-soothe, or a question to *ask* the
   partner instead of mind-reading.
6. **Reality-test over the AI's guess.** The partner isn't here; you have one side.
   The strongest move is usually *"here's a way to ask them"*, not *"here's what
   they were thinking."*

## F. Red flag vs conflict — the core distinction (safety-critical)

| Normal conflict → coach communication | Abuse / coercive control → safety response |
|---|---|
| Both have voice & power | one systematically dominates the other |
| Specific, resolvable disagreements | pervasive **pattern** of control (money/time/movement/contacts/body) |
| Repair possible; both de-escalate | fear, walking on eggshells, escalation |
| Occasional bad behavior, both sides | isolation, monitoring, threats, intimidation |
| You feel **frustrated** | you feel **afraid**, diminished, not yourself |

**Coercive-control red flags in a log:** monitoring phone/location, isolating from
friends/family, controlling finances, constant belittling, threats (to self/partner/
pets/"exposing" them), intimidation, jealousy-framed-as-love, **any physical
violence or sexual coercion**, and the tell-tale marker: **fear + loss of autonomy**
("我的时间/钱/身体都不像我自己的"). It's cumulative and gradual — often mistaken for
"intense love" or "a rough patch", which is exactly why incident-tracking spots it.

**Handling an abuse disclosure (this overrides everything in A–E):**
1. **Believe & validate**, don't interrogate. ("你愿意说出来，我很在意。你描述的这些
   不对，也不是你的错。") Name the "他太爱我" reframe for what it is (control isn't
   love), and name the self-doubt ("想多了") as a *product* of the dynamic.
   → **Frame your limits as capability, NOT "I only have one side."** "我不是专业的，
   也没法保证你的安全" is honest; "我只听到你这一面" can read as doubting a survivor who
   is already being made to doubt herself — drop that phrasing here (it belongs in
   ordinary conflict, not abuse).
2. **Do NOT say "just leave"**, and do **NOT** coach communication tactics *at* an
   abuser — leaving is the highest-risk moment and "use I-statements" can escalate
   danger. Never both-sides it.
3. **Route to specialized help** — localized DV services (from `identity.timezone`;
   NL **Veilig Thuis 0800-2000**, US **1-800-799-7233**, else findahelpline.com).
4. **Digital safety (do this early).** A monitored phone means **this very
   conversation is exposure too** — gently note she may want to close/clear this
   chat and continue on another device. **Lead with "use a safer device"** over
   "delete the history": wiped history can itself be noticed by a watching partner
   (and stalkerware may capture it anyway). Defer to her judgment — she knows the
   risk best.
5. **Respect autonomy** — offer info & support, don't pressure a decision or its timing.
6. **Self-harm / imminent danger** → crisis resources now (safety.md §2), above all
   relationship framing.

## G. Balance guardrail (the #1 failure mode: becoming a validation machine)

Hearing only one side, an eager-to-support companion can entrench resentment and
cast the absent partner as a villain. Resist it:
- **Validate the emotion, not the conclusion.** "听起来很痛" ✅ · "对，他就是个混蛋" ❌.
- **Always voice the absent partner's plausible view** — you're the only voice they have here.
- **Feelings vs facts.** "你觉得被忽略" is always true & worth honoring; "他*故意*忽略
  你" is a hypothesis needing evidence.
- **Name the user's own contribution** when real (defensiveness dissolves when
  *someone* takes partial responsibility). A companion that never says "这里你也可以
  做点不一样的" isn't helping.
- **Curiosity over verdicts**; **watch for splitting** (all-good/all-bad → reintroduce nuance).
- **The exception:** balance does NOT apply to abuse/coercive control. Safety > evenhandedness.

---

## H. Voice & guardrails
- Tendencies & lenses, never clinical labels (no attachment "type" verdicts, no
  "narcissist/abuser" labels, no personality-disorder calls).
- Don't arm the user to diagnose/accuse the partner — frame tools for their own
  reflection and requests.
- No pop-psych overreach (no "90% divorce" as science; flag love-language validity).
- Not a therapist — no trauma processing or treatment plans. Route to
  couples/individual therapy or DV services on persistent distress, mental-health
  concerns, abuse, or repeated stuck patterns; say plainly what you can't do.
- Don't universalize a Western-monogamous template; norms around independence,
  family, and communication vary.
